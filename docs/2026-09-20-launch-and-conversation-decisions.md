# Potluck — saved launch and conversation decisions

Recorded September 20, 2026 at the user's explicit request. Later decisions below supersede conflicting earlier suggestions. This is a decision record, not implementation or legal clearance.

## Launch and development sequence

**Launch Potluck as Splitfinder for housing and bills.** Bills includes finding people for subscriptions and memberships; it is not limited to roommate-related bills. Start campus by campus, with Georgia Tech as the initial community. This supersedes descriptions of banking and virtual cards as launch prerequisites.

1. Initial product: discovery, listings, search, profiles, inquiries and connecting people who already have or want a shared arrangement. A forum is not required. No in-app money movement, virtual cards, pooled funds, bank integration or automated collection is promised at launch.
2. Expand toward ongoing group/Circle and shared-bill coordination as separately scoped. Exact intermediate features and release order remain open; reimbursement ledgers or external payment integrations are not approved merely by this sequence.
3. Full financial Potluck remains the destination: host-controlled cards, contributor funding and recurring agreements, bill execution, spending permissions and controls. Funding, issuer/bank approval, supported provider capabilities, consent, security and operational readiness are prerequisites. Existing financial specifications remain future scope, not evidence of provider approval.

Experiences, concerts, campus events and parties are possible later expansion, not the housing-and-bills launch commitment. Preserve shared-purpose discovery rather than assuming a pivot into a generic campus event feed. For later experiences, find an event then people attached to it; exact aggregation of events and invitations remains open.

The full-product Circles / Cards / Bills / Splitfinder navigation is not automatically the discovery-only launch shell. Initial navigation, monetization, exact launch feature cut and intermediate coordination scope require decisions.

## Residential decisions

- Working concept: **I'm looking to split this**. Users choose **1–3 properties**, each with its own price and roommates needed.
- Each property is an individual searchable post. Search should not reveal the poster's other options. Alternative posts can be grouped on the public profile. They represent alternative homes, not multiple commitments.
- Anyone may post, including prospective roommates, existing tenants, landlords, agents and apartment communities. Apartment partnerships are future ambitions.
- **Only residential posters require ID verification.** Inquiry senders can DM immediately without an acceptance gate or discovery ID requirement. This supersedes the earlier message-sender verification idea. Do not automatically extend this policy to every bill-listing subtype.
- Require verification at Publish, after drafting. Retain the draft while unverified posters verify; verified posters publish directly. No upfront ID disclaimer during composition/review. No publication before verification.
- Required established inputs: address, rent, roommates needed, lease length. Introduction and website optional; original listing URL not required.
- Exact move-in timing is a feature, including search filtering; this supersedes messages-only timing. Whether a date must always be mandatory remains open.
- Beds, baths and roommates use vertical **up arrow / number / down arrow** controls.
- Show total rent and automatically estimated per-person share with poster override. Default denominator remains unresolved; roommates needed is not necessarily total occupants, and a landlord is not necessarily an occupant.
- Posts are public and visible on profiles. Exact public address visibility remains open; collection does not imply approval to expose it precisely.
- Post-management cards display the primary photo. Posters manually take down posts when roommates are found; no required automatic vacancy countdown or closure.
- Introductions/planning only: no applications, leases, deposits or purchases in this flow. Later Potluck cards/bills may be recommended, never forced. An inquiry is not a financial agreement, Circle membership or spending grant.

## Branding research saved from September 19, 2026

This is U.S.-focused research, not legal clearance or a finalized logo policy. Recheck sources before launch. Answer brand-identification questions directly; do not repeatedly substitute subscription eligibility discussion. Platform status does not establish immunity.

- **15 USC 1114(1), 1125(a)(1)(A):** names and logos can be trademarks; likely confusion about source, affiliation, sponsorship or approval matters. Lack of individual permission is not by itself every element of infringement. Truthful identification can be lawful without a bespoke license.
- Nominative fair use may protect identification. Tests differ by jurisdiction. Ninth Circuit analysis considers identifying necessity, amount used and implied endorsement; it is not a nationwide automatic safe harbor.
- **Toyota v. Tabari, 610 F.3d 1171 (9th Cir. 2010):** distinguished permissible Lexus name identification from stylized branding/logo use that was unnecessary and potentially misleading in that setting. It does not ban every third-party logo.
- **Parsons v. Regna, March 17, 2021 (11th Cir., unpublished):** left formal treatment of the doctrine unresolved and rejected the proposed defense on the facts. Do not present it as binding nationwide or a complete current circuit-law survey.
- Names/short phrases are not copyrightable; sufficiently original logo artwork may be. **17 USC 106/107** concern copying/display and copyright fair use, separate from trademark fair use. Small size, commercial use, an unmodified logo or disclaimer alone does not settle legality.
- **1125(c)(3)(A)** has a nominative/descriptive fair-use exclusion for dilution, not universal immunity from infringement.
- Permission can be published conditional terms, an integration agreement or an individual license. Brand guidelines are not statutes; available downloads are not unlimited permission, and restrictive guidelines do not erase otherwise lawful uses.
- OpenAI publishes conditional brand permissions tied to its services and presentation/no-endorsement rules; permission is revocable. Spotify developer mark permission is scoped to use/results of Spotify Platform use, not a general unrelated group-directory license. Apple general guidelines distinguish referential name uses from logo/graphic uses generally requiring authorization. Check exact asset-specific terms.
- App Store **5.2/5.2.1** are separate distribution rules. Legal defensibility does not guarantee approval; rejection is not proof of infringement. Third-party brands are covered too.
- Rocket Money's OpenAI merchant identifier screenshot does not establish its legal basis. Do not invent a special partnership or exemption for large apps.
- Names in Potluck typography as default was an **assistant recommendation, not a locked user decision**. Logos require individual assessment under permission or a defensible legal basis. Neither all logos forbidden nor all identifiers automatically allowed is accurate.
- Brand use and service-plan eligibility are separate issues. This conversation does not approve violating sharing terms or establish service-by-service eligibility.
